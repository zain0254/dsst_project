# Final Results and Methodological Audit

## Research Title

**Robust Adaptive Thresholding for LSTM Autoencoder-Based Anomaly Detection in Streaming Time Series**

**Repository:** `dsst_project`

## 1. Purpose and Scope

This report documents the completed primary benchmark, its experimental protocol, quantitative results, statistical hypothesis evaluation, verification findings, and methodological limitations.

The study investigates whether a moving-median plus Median Absolute Deviation (MAD) threshold can reduce false-positive alarms when applied to reconstruction errors generated by an LSTM Autoencoder.

The primary benchmark evaluates four thresholding methods across 23 selected real-world datasets from the Numenta Anomaly Benchmark (NAB). Seventeen datasets have complete positive anomaly events in the held-out test partition; six have no positive test labels and are retained as negative-only test cases for supplementary false-alarm analysis.

An earlier experiment, referred to as V1, is preserved as historical evidence. The earlier and primary experiments use different protocols and must not be combined into a single aggregate result.

**Principal research finding:** The proposed MAD threshold substantially reduced mean false-positive rate relative to the two fixed baselines. However, it also produced lower mean F1 and event recall. The results therefore demonstrate a trade-off rather than overall superiority.

## 4. Thresholding Methods

Four thresholding approaches are evaluated.

### 4.1 Fixed Percentile

Implementation identifier:

`fixed_percentile`

This method uses a fixed percentile of reconstruction errors to determine the anomaly threshold.

### 4.2 Fixed Mean Plus Standard Deviations

Implementation identifier:

`fixed_mean_k_sigma`

This method calculates a threshold using the mean reconstruction error and a configurable multiple of its standard deviation.

### 4.3 Moving Median Plus MAD — Proposed Method

Implementation identifier:

`moving_median_lambda_mad`

The proposed method uses a moving median and Median Absolute Deviation to adapt the reconstruction-error threshold.

The motivation is to obtain a robust threshold under changing error distributions and reduce sensitivity to unusually large reconstruction errors.

### 4.4 Rolling Mean Plus k Standard Deviations (Validation-Calibrated)

Implementation identifier:

`rolling_mean_3sigma`

This method calculates a time-dependent threshold using rolling historical mean and standard deviation. Its multiplier k is selected from the same candidate grid as the fixed mean-plus-k-sigma baseline using validation labels and the shared 5% validation-FPR target. It is not a fixed 3-sigma threshold.

The implementation is causal: the historical rolling calculation excludes the current reconstruction error.

## 5. Primary Benchmark Results

The completed benchmark produced the following aggregate results:

| Threshold method | Mean F1 | Mean FPR | Mean event recall | Mean NAB-style score |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Fixed mean + kσ | 0.309673 | 0.134183 | 0.957516 | -330.948530 |
| Fixed percentile | 0.307111 | 0.138636 | 0.968410 | -368.674679 |
| Proposed moving median + MAD | 0.128007 | 0.029905 | 0.697168 | -27.367282 |
| Rolling mean + kσ (validation-calibrated) | 0.048590 | 0.019042 | 0.789760 | -23.640771 |

Mean F1 and FPR are reported across the 23-dataset benchmark. Event recall, event delay, and NAB-style test-partition score use the 17 event-evaluable datasets. High-variance FPR uses 20 datasets with at least one normal high-variance test point. ROC-AUC and PR-AUC are defined only where both classes occur in the test partition.

The detailed output tables should be used when reporting metric-specific coverage.

### 5.1 False-Positive Reduction

The proposed MAD threshold achieved a substantially lower mean FPR than the two fixed baselines.

Compared with fixed percentile:

\[
\frac{0.138636-0.029905}{0.138636}\times100
\approx 78.4\%
\]

Compared with fixed mean plus kσ:

\[
\frac{0.134183-0.029905}{0.134183}\times100
\approx 77.7\%
\]

This is the principal favorable result for the proposed method.

### 5.2 F1 Performance

The proposed method achieved mean F1 of 0.128007.

The fixed baselines achieved:

- Fixed percentile: 0.307111.
- Fixed mean plus kσ: 0.309673.

The proposed method therefore did not achieve the highest mean F1.

The results indicate that reducing false-positive alarms was accompanied by a reduction in overall point-level detection performance.

### 5.3 Event Recall

The proposed method achieved mean event recall of 0.697168.

The fixed baselines achieved:

- Fixed percentile: 0.968410.
- Fixed mean plus kσ: 0.957516.

The proposed method therefore missed more test events on average than either fixed baseline in the reported event-evaluable cohort.

### 5.4 Event-Evaluable Cohort

The following results are reported for the 17 event-evaluable datasets:

| Method | Mean F1 | Mean FPR | Event recall | Delay fraction |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Fixed mean + kσ | 0.418970 | 0.168924 | 0.957516 | 0.433546 |
| Fixed percentile | 0.415503 | 0.170556 | 0.968410 | 0.439675 |

The proposed method's delay fraction is higher than both fixed baselines. Therefore, the results do not support a claim that it detects anomalies earlier than those methods.

The rolling mean method has the lowest delay fraction in this comparison, but also the lowest event-evaluable mean F1.

## 6. Statistical Hypothesis Evaluation

The project evaluates three research hypotheses.

### 6.1 H1 — Improvement in F1

**Outcome: Not supported.**

The statistical comparisons use 17 paired event-evaluable datasets.

| Comparison | Proposed minus baseline mean F1 | Wilcoxon p-value | Holm-adjusted p-value |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Proposed MAD vs fixed percentile | -0.242316 | 0.000015 | 0.000046 |
| Proposed MAD vs fixed mean + kσ | -0.245783 | 0.000015 | 0.000046 |

The fixed baselines performed better on all 17 paired datasets in both comparisons.

The adjusted p-values indicate statistically significant differences in favor of the fixed baselines, not in favor of the proposed method.

Consequently, the hypothesis that the proposed method improves F1 is not supported.

### 6.2 H2 — False-Positive Reduction

**Outcome: Dataset-level improvements observed; aggregate evidence inconclusive after Holm correction.**

| Comparison | Proposed minus baseline mean FPR | Paired t-test p-value | Holm-adjusted p-value |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Proposed MAD vs fixed percentile | -0.147631 | 0.021253 | 0.063758 |
| Proposed MAD vs fixed mean + kσ | -0.138423 | 0.025440 | 0.063758 |
| Proposed MAD vs rolling mean + kσ | 0.041851 | 0.069577 | 0.069577 |

The clean-normal volatility reanalysis uses 20 paired datasets. The first two unadjusted p-values are below 0.05, but none of the Holm-adjusted p-values is below 0.05. Therefore, the aggregate statistical evidence does not establish H2 at the 0.05 significance level after multiple-comparison correction.

The lower mean FPR remains a descriptive result of the benchmark, but it should not be presented as statistically significant aggregate evidence after correction.

### 6.3 H3 — NAB-Style Score, False Alerts, and Detection Timing

**Outcome: Descriptive only; not statistically established.**

On the 17 event-evaluable datasets, the proposed method's mean NAB-style test-partition score was -27.367, compared with -368.675 for fixed percentile, -330.949 for fixed mean + kσ, and -23.641 for rolling mean + kσ. The paired differences versus the two fixed baselines were positive but not statistically significant after Holm correction (adjusted p = 0.264740 and 0.191483). The proposed method did not outperform the rolling comparator (mean difference -3.727; adjusted p = 0.264740).

The proposed method also had lower event recall than the fixed baselines and a higher mean detection-delay fraction than all three comparators. Its more favorable score relative to the fixed methods is therefore a descriptive false-alarm trade-off, not evidence of overall superiority or earlier detection. H3 should not be reported as statistically confirmed.

## 8. Methodological Limitations and Label Use

The primary benchmark must not be described as completely label-free unsupervised learning.

### 8.1 Normal-Only Training

NAB labels are used to exclude anomaly-touching windows from the training data.

The scaler is fitted on normal training rows only.

This creates a label-informed normal-only training protocol.

### 8.2 Validation-Based Threshold Calibration

Validation labels are used during threshold selection and validation-FPR-budget evaluation.

The configured validation FPR budget is 5%.

This is a target, not a guaranteed upper bound. When no available candidate satisfies the budget, the tuner minimizes FPR among the available candidates.

### 8.3 Test-Label-Informed Cohort Selection

Test-label properties are used in dataset eligibility and cohort selection.

This introduces potential cohort-selection bias.

Although model fitting and threshold tuning do not use test labels, the selection of datasets depends on properties of the test labels.

The conclusions are therefore conditional on the selected cohort and should not be generalized to all time-series datasets without further evaluation.

### 8.4 Custom NAB-Style Score

The reported NAB-style score is a project-specific score calculated on the chronological test partition.

It is not the official NAB full-corpus leaderboard score.

A better custom score should not be interpreted independently of false-positive rate, event recall, and detection delay. It does not automatically mean that more events were detected or that detection happened earlier.

## 10. Floating-Point Reporting Correction

A numerical-tie issue was identified in pairwise directional reporting.

An extremely small floating-point difference, approximately:

`-4.45607092891542e-17`

could previously be treated as a strict improvement or regression instead of a numerical tie.

The reporting implementation now applies an absolute numerical-zero tolerance of:

`1e-12`

Near-zero differences are canonicalized before the relevant Wilcoxon and directional-count calculations.

A regression test was added:

`test_pairwise_counts_roundoff_difference_as_single_tie`

After the correction:

- 20 automated tests passed.
- 10 out of 10 report files passed the original-versus-corrected audit.
- All 8 statistical output rows were independently reproduced.
- The statistical output reproduction audit passed.
- No near-zero paired differences remained in the audited statistical output.

The corrected reporting outputs are in:

`source_code/outputs/version2_normal_only_fpr05_reporting_corrected_20261115/`

The original raw benchmark evidence remains in:

`source_code/outputs/version2_normal_only_fpr05/`

This correction changed pairwise reporting calculations only. It did not change model weights, training, saved predictions, or raw metric values.

### 10.1 Clean-Normal Volatility Reanalysis

A separate review found that the original high-variance reference cutoff included training histories containing labeled anomalies. The reviewed implementation now calculates the 75th-percentile volatility cutoff from full trailing training histories whose historical points are all labeled normal. The current point remains excluded from each history window.

The saved 207 prediction files were re-evaluated without retraining. All 828 method-level metric rows were recalculated. Only `high_variance_fpr` and `high_variance_normal_points` changed; every other metric reproduced within an absolute difference of 4.6e-13. The H2 comparison now uses 20 paired datasets. Its Holm-adjusted p-values are 0.063758, 0.063758, and 0.069577, so the aggregate evidence remains inconclusive at alpha = 0.05.

The macro dataset means for high-variance FPR are 0.223287 (fixed percentile), 0.214080 (fixed mean + kσ), 0.075657 (proposed MAD), and 0.033806 (rolling mean + kσ). The proposed method therefore reduces high-variance FPR relative to the two fixed baselines but remains above the rolling adaptive baseline.

The updated metrics, tables, plots, and audit record are in. The original prediction traces remain unchanged in `source_code/outputs/version2_normal_only_fpr05/predictions/` and are read by the reanalysis script:

## 13. Overall Research Interpretation

The completed primary benchmark shows that moving-median plus MAD thresholding can substantially reduce mean false-positive rate compared with fixed-percentile and fixed mean-plus-kσ thresholds on the selected NAB cohort.

However, this reduction is accompanied by lower mean F1 and event recall.

The proposed method also does not demonstrate an earlier-detection advantage over the fixed baselines.

The hypothesis outcomes are:

- **H1 — F1 improvement:** Not supported.
- **H2 — False-positive reduction:** Dataset-level improvements observed; aggregate evidence inconclusive after Holm correction.
- **H3 — Earlier detection:** Descriptive only.

These results suggest that adaptive thresholding may be useful when false-positive reduction is the primary objective, but its detection-coverage trade-off requires further investigation.

The evidence does not establish that the proposed method is universally superior or ready for production use.

---

## 14. Recommended Future Research

Further research should investigate:

1. Improving the trade-off between false-positive reduction and event recall.
2. Separating dataset selection and threshold calibration from final test evaluation.
3. Selecting evaluation datasets without using test-label outcomes.
4. Testing generalization on additional time-series datasets.
5. Studying sensitivity to window size, random seed, and threshold hyperparameters.
6. Comparing adaptive methods under consistent false-positive constraints.
7. Conducting additional event-level statistical analysis with a clearly defined independent evaluation cohort.

These directions would strengthen the methodology and provide a more defensible assessment of adaptive thresholding.

---

## 15. Final Conclusion

The completed primary benchmark provides a reproducible comparison of four reconstruction-error thresholding methods across 23 NAB datasets.

The proposed moving-median plus MAD method achieved a substantial reduction in mean false-positive rate relative to the two fixed baselines, but this came at the cost of lower mean F1 and event recall.

The statistical findings do not support the proposed method's F1-improvement hypothesis. Aggregate evidence for false-positive reduction is inconclusive after Holm correction, and earlier detection has not been established inferentially.

The experiment also has important methodological constraints involving label-informed training, validation calibration, dataset selection, and custom NAB-style scoring. These limitations are explicitly retained in the research record.

The appropriate conclusion is that the proposed adaptive threshold shows a promising false-alarm reduction characteristic on the selected cohort, but further work is needed to improve detection coverage and establish generalization.

**This report records the observed results and limitations rather than presenting the proposed approach as a proven superior method.**
